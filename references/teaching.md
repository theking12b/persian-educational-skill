# Teaching from a raw source

Keep a working coverage ledger: source page/range, claim/figure/example, destination and decision. Each in-scope page needs a destination or a specific reason for merging it. Account for captions, footnotes, boundary cases, instructor notes and meaningful animation steps. A reference handout supplies style, while the raw source governs subject coverage.

Read figures at full size. Extracted text can reverse signs, lose exponents or jumble Persian order. For scans, read the rendered image or use local OCR with the appropriate languages (for example Tesseract fas+eng). Compare recovered text against the image. For an unreadable item record the page and uncertainty and avoid inventing the missing claim.

Begin a learning unit with a question the reader can answer by its end. Introduce prerequisites at first use, explain a mechanism in causal steps, and trace one example from assumptions through result to interpretation. Label added examples and assumptions in the lesson language. Map the parts of an analogy back to the formal concept. Use a boundary case when it changes the result. For a simple definition two sentences may suffice.

Check novel claims by deriving them with stated assumptions or citing an approved authoritative source. Document conflicts with the slides. Transcribe formulas visually; check domain, units, indexes, minus signs, limits and inequality direction. Substitute an ordinary and a boundary value. Trace an algorithm or execute a small example, enumerate truth-table cases, check probability totals, distinguish necessary from sufficient conditions. Compare labels, values and units across prose, equations, diagrams and tables.
